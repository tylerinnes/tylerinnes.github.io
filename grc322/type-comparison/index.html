Futura vs. Mr Eaves Mod

History of Each Typeface

Futura

Futura is a geometric sans-serif typeface created in Germany in the 1920s. It was designed by Paul Renner and released in 1927 by the Bauer Type Foundry. Although Renner was not directly associated with the Bauhaus school, Futura reflects the same modernist ethos by reducing letterforms to pure geometric shapes and avoiding ornamental details.

Renner began sketching the concept in 1920, and the final design emerged as part of a broader European movement toward functional and rational design. Futura quickly became a widely adopted modern typeface, used extensively in advertising, publishing, and branding. Its clean forms make it especially effective for headings and signage.

One of the most historically significant uses of Futura was its appearance on the Apollo 11 lunar plaque in 1969, symbolizing industrial and technological achievement. Over time, Futura has expanded into numerous variations—including condensed, bold, and display styles—and has influenced countless geometric sans-serif typefaces throughout the 20th century. Notable uses include Supreme, Party City, Volkswagen, Royal Dutch Shell, Crayola, FremantleMedia, and HP.

Mr Eaves Mod

Mr Eaves Modern is a sans-serif typeface designed by Zuzana Licko and released in 2009 by Emigre Fonts, the influential independent digital type foundry based in Berkeley, California. It is a sans-serif companion to Mrs Eaves, which itself is inspired by Baskerville, originally designed in 18th-century England.

Rather than simply removing serifs from Mrs Eaves, Licko developed Mr Eaves with its own typographic identity while maintaining proportional harmony with its serif counterpart. The Mr Eaves family includes both Mr Eaves Sans, which retains humanistic characteristics, and Mr Eaves Modern, which adopts a cleaner, more geometric appearance with squared terminals and simplified counters.

Unlike Futura’s strong association with avant-garde modernism, Mr Eaves Modern was designed as a flexible and well-balanced sans-serif suitable for editorial, corporate, and branding applications. Its clear spacing and refined letterforms allow it to perform well in both print and digital environments, making it a professional and understated modern typeface.
